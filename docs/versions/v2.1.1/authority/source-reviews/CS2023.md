## 2. Authority fit

CS2023 is a jointly produced Computer Science curriculum framework. It is primary evidence for the curriculum knowledge model, the curriculum's 17 Knowledge Areas, and the Software Engineering Knowledge Units as presented in that report.

It is useful for:
- distinguishing Software Engineering education from the broader Computer Science curriculum;
- identifying educationally relevant knowledge units and adjacent areas;
- observing how curriculum topics are cross-referenced across Knowledge Areas.

It is not:
- a complete professional Software Engineering taxonomy;
- a universal professional competency or certification standard;
- empirical evidence that a teaching topic or practice is effective in every engineering context.

## 4. Important modeling implications

1. The 17 KAs belong to a Computer Science curriculum knowledge model. Software Engineering is one KA within that wider educational model.
2. A Knowledge Unit count is a curriculum design fact, not a measure of the complete professional domain.
3. CS2023 explicitly describes Knowledge Areas as non-insular and notes that cross-KA references can represent different perspectives on shared topics. This is direct support for retaining cross-links rather than forcing all knowledge into mutually exclusive branches.
4. The SE curriculum is intentionally selective and targeted at graduate preparation. It emphasizes teamwork, tools, requirements, design, construction, verification/validation, evolution, reliability and formal methods.
5. The SE-Tools unit includes version control, release management, CI, testing tools and dependency management. This is a curriculum observation; it does not establish that these topics exhaust Software Engineering Operations or Configuration Management.
